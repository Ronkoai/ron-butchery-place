Meat, written down

A long-form project on the history and culture of meat — from the domestication of cattle and pigs to why Korean cooks cut beef thin, grill it over live fire, and eat the animal down to the last layer.

The claim is not that one country's beef is best. It is that the way a people cut, cook and eat an animal carries its history, and that the record deserves to be read closely rather than repeated.

Research Seven papers completed and organised by theme — breeds and native stock, pre-modern Korean pork, old records, the “wagyu origin” and “samgyeopsal export” stories checked against sources, butchery and cooking methods.
Manuscript Introduction and prologue drafted and being revised by hand, section by section.
